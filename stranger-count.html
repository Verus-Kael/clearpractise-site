Paste your homepage HTML and see the third-party domains referenced directly by your page. Runs entirely in your browser - nothing is uploaded, stored or sent.

In November 2026, 178 major websites - the BBC, CNN, HSBC - served malicious code to their visitors. None of them were hacked. The code arrived through third-party scripts they had chosen to trust. (Read the full story.) Every external domain involved in delivering your page is a dependency. Some just serve images; others can execute code inside it. The more access a dependency has, the more its security becomes your security. This tool counts them.

What this test can see: third-party resources referenced directly in the HTML you pasted, including scripts, embeds, media, stylesheets and fonts. What it cannot see: network requests created later by JavaScript, or additional resources loaded by third-party scripts and stylesheets. For that, you need a live network scan.

How to read the results

Scripts can run arbitrary code on your pages. This is the category that delivered the November 2026 supply-chain attack - a stranger with script access is a stranger who can change what your website does, without you ever knowing.

Embedded content places other sites' code inside yours, running under your name.

Media and other resources mean your visitors' browsers contact another organisation's server when the page loads - a real dependency, even when it is just a hosted image.

Stylesheets and fonts are the lowest-risk category, but still mean someone else's server is involved every time your page loads.

A high count is not automatically shameful - some businesses genuinely need a handful of third parties. But most sites carry more than they think, and every one is a dependency you must trust forever. Fewer dependencies, fewer doors.
